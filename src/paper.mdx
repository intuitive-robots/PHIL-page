## Abstract

Proactive Human-in-the-Loop (PHIL) is a framework for proactive policy correction and preference-based post-training. It lets operators preview policy-predicted future motion and intervene before anticipated undesirable actions. During takeover, PHIL continues querying the policy and displaying its predicted trajectories in the physical workspace through an extended reality (XR) interface. PHIL pairs rejected policy predictions with corrective action chunks subsequently executed from the same physical state over the same horizon, and preference-based post-training encourages the policy to favor these corrections.

We evaluate PHIL on real-world manipulation with a diffusion policy and a pretrained vision-language-action policy. With five correction episodes per condition, PHIL increases mean success rates from **44.0% to 70.3% for Diffusion Policy** and from **57.8% to 76.9% for X-VLA**, compared with HG-DAgger. These are unweighted means across each policy’s evaluated tasks and demonstration budgets: four tasks for Diffusion Policy and three for X-VLA. Separately, proactive-trained policies show fewer unnecessary recovery behaviors in successful episodes.

### Reactive vs. proactive correction

Reactive correction responds after an error. PHIL previews future motion, allowing the operator to intervene before an anticipated undesirable action. The clip below illustrates the difference.

### Proactive human intervention in XR

A Meta Quest 3 interface aligns predicted end-effector trajectories with the physical workspace. Holding the controller trigger enables correction; releasing it returns control to the policy. Predictions continue to update during human takeover.

<Video src={asset("media/clips/xr-intervention.mp4")} poster={asset("media/clips/xr-intervention.jpg")} autoplay={false} loop={false} preload="none" aria-label="Proactive human intervention using XR trajectory previews" />

### Preference learning from corrections

PHIL pairs a rejected prediction with the next executed corrective action chunk, starting from the same query state and spanning the same horizon. Post-training combines imitation on the original demonstrations with a preference objective that favors the corrective continuation.

<Video src={asset("media/clips/preference-learning.mp4")} poster={asset("media/clips/preference-learning.jpg")} autoplay={false} loop={false} preload="none" aria-label="Construction of rejected and corrected action pairs for preference learning" />

The positive chunk may extend beyond the end of takeover; the prediction accepted at release is excluded from negative samples. Only complete pairs are used for training. Actions run at 20 Hz, with a 30-step prediction horizon and a query every 10 control steps.

## Experiments and results

Each condition uses **five correction rollout episodes** and **25 evaluation rollouts**. Multiple interventions are allowed within a correction episode. Diffusion Policy is evaluated on Pick Up Banana, Towel Folding, Cook the Carrot, and Lemon in the Drawer. X-VLA is evaluated on the latter three tasks. Base-demonstration budgets are 3/6/9 for Diffusion Policy on Pick Up Banana, and 10/20/30 elsewhere.

| Policy | HG-DAgger | PHIL | Evaluation scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diffusion Policy | 44.0% | **70.3%** | 4 tasks × 3 demonstration budgets |
| X-VLA | 57.8% | **76.9%** | 3 tasks × 3 demonstration budgets |

Mean full-task success rates, averaged over each policy’s evaluated tasks and demonstration budgets. HG-DAgger uses reactive corrections; PHIL uses proactive corrections.

### Correction collection and post-training

Proactive collection alone does not consistently improve success under behavior cloning. On the same proactive data, preference post-training matches or improves on HG-DAgger across all four tasks in the lowest-data regime.

## Takeaways

- **Preview before intervening.** XR makes a policy’s proposed motion visible before it is executed.
- **Learn from the comparison.** Rejected predictions and executed corrections provide context-aligned preference pairs.
- **Improve with a small correction budget.** Five correction episodes improve mean success over HG-DAgger in the evaluated Diffusion Policy and X-VLA settings.
